OLDHAM COUNTY, Ky. — A proposal to develop a massive data center on farmland along Highway 53 near La Grange is sparking debate and backlash. The two-million-square-foot project, proposed by Western Hospitality Partners, has drawn criticism from local residents concerned about its potential to alter the rural landscape and the alleged bypassing of planning and zoning processes. It was to take it out of the hands of elected officials," said Sarah Little, who opposes the data center. During a packed meeting with Oldham County's Technical Review Committee, residents questioned the site's conditional use permit filing, expressing concerns about property values, transparency, and energy requirements. Critics argue that the project strategically avoided the re-zoning process by designating itself as a private utility. "Zoning would all need to be adjusted. And there's really not a place that I've been able to see that is already prepared to welcome a data center, especially considering the size they need a tremendous amount of property, they need a tremendous amount of energy," said Sen. Lindsey Tichenor. Some residents, like Dave Oberg, fear a significant drop in home values, which could create financial hardships, particularly for retirees living near the site. A group called "We Are Oldham County" has vocally opposed the development, emphasizing the lack of transparency. Defending the proposal, developer representative Cliff Ashburner stated efforts have been made to maintain open communication, including community meetings and a project website. The final decision on the proposal is anticipated in June, pending the Board of Adjustments' review.
