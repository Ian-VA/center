The first hyperscale data center in the area took another step forward this week with its preferred site being annexed into the city of Sioux Falls — but only after the company behind the project promised taxpayers won’t foot any of the bill. City councilors on Tuesday signed off on bringing into city limits the swath of land near Rice Street and Veterans Parkway that could host servers for some of the world’s most prominent tech companies. ADVERTISEMENT Annexed at the request of landowner Gemini Data Center SD LLC, the cropland is not contiguous with Sioux Falls city limits, and the company had to promise to cover the cost of extending utilities to the site in order to earn the blessing of both the mayor’s office and city councilors. “Any future property owner, if Gemini would walk away, would get that notice of claim that indicates that they are responsible for all the future improvements with water, sewer and roads,” said Jason Bieber, a longtime planning staffer in the city’s Planning and Development Services Department. While being part of the city comes with higher tax burdens, datacenter reliance on water, sewer and electricity means being able to tap into city utilities made the project more viable, according to project backers. The site’s proximity to two major power stations, which could support a 500-megawatt load, also made it appealing to Gemini, which purchased the property from Xcel Energy in 2024. “With access to the city’s reliable utility infrastructure, the site is now even better positioned to host a potential data center — which we’ve consistently identified as its highest and best use,” said Kyle Peters, a partner with A1 Development Solutions, which is helping California-based Gemini Capital navigate local and state regulations.
