Weyerhaeuser NR Company has requested rezoning of 291.5 acres from agricultural to general industrial use for the data center project on Highway 129 in Dry Branch. The Twiggs County Board of Commissioners will hold a public hearing on the proposal Thursday, Sept. 18, at the county courthouse. County Commission Chairman Ken Fowler said the project could significantly boost the county's tax base, though specific revenue figures have not been disclosed. "They're telling me this will triple our tax base," Fowler said. "I think everybody's in bed with it because of the money side." However, Fowler said he lacks detailed information about potential environmental impacts on county residents. "Now, what we do to the environment or how it affects the citizens of the county, I have no idea," he said. "I don't have enough information really to talk about that." The proposal has raised concerns among nearby residents, particularly about water usage and quality. Nancy Lubeck, who lives about a mile from the proposed site, said she and her neighbors rely on private wells and worry about the data center's impact on their water supply. "We're very worried," Lubeck said. "From what we understand, they will be drilling wells. There's a lot of unanswered questions that nobody seems to have a definite answer on." Lubeck said residents discovered the proposal only after Fowler contacted them, raising questions about transparency in the process. "Our county commission chair called us and told us — he was the only commissioner that bothered to call and tell us that this was going on," she said. "That was actually our first red flag. Why hasn't anybody else told us about this?" The project follows a broader trend of data center development across Georgia, where counties are converting agricultural land for major technology campuses. Monroe County recently approved one data center project while rejecting another after community opposition. Fowler indicated that his fellow commissioners appear supportive of the Twiggs County project, saying "all my fellow commissioners are already in bed with it" based on conversations he's had. The data center would be built on a portion of an 890-acre tract currently owned by Weyerhaeuser, a timber company. The Lubeck’s worry about potential expansion beyond the initial 291.5 acres requested for rezoning. "Is it, I want to rezone this 291 today and, you know, another year down the road or a few months down the road, are they gonna come back and want another 300 acres?" Lubeck asked. Additional concerns include potential impacts on nearby Robins Air Force Base operations and increases in local electricity costs. The project timeline and job creation numbers have not been publicly disclosed. Data centers typically require massive investments but create relatively few permanent jobs due to automation. State oversight of data center projects has been temporarily suspended while Georgia's Department of Community Affairs conducts a study on how to better evaluate such developments. Weyerhaeuser did not respond to requests for comment by publication time. The September public hearing will provide the first official forum for community input on the proposal. If approved by a majority of the five-member commission, the rezoning would clear a major hurdle for the project to proceed to the permitting phase.
