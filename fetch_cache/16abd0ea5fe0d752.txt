Beverly Bragg/Franklin Favorite Facebook Twitter WhatsApp SMS Email Print Copy article link Save A proposal for a data center was discussed at length during a contentious meeting held in the conference room of the Franklin Police Station this week. After reviewing a revised preliminary development plan submitted by TenKey LandCo, LLC, for a data storage and service center with power manufacturing to be built on Steele Road in Franklin, the Franklin Planning & Zoning Commission voted to approve the preliminary development plan. The vote was unanimous. The approval is contingent on TenKey providing a monthly status report to the P&Z commission on aspects of the project as it moves forward. According to TenKey attorney Greg Dutton, these reports will be made available to the public as well. A data center is a specialized facility that houses a vast array of computer systems, servers, and networking equipment to store, process, and manage digital data and applications. These facilities often utilize on-site power generation systems, modular energy systems, gas turbine arrays, fuel cells, and battery energy storage. The data storage and service center proposed by TenKey is comprised of three 200,000-square-foot facilities developed over a three-year period of time on approximately 200 acres located near Exit 2 off Interstate 65 in Franklin. A preliminary development plan presented by the company on Feb. 5 was tabled by the P&Z commission. The meeting was packed to capacity with standing-room-only attendance as members of the P&Z commission asked TenKey representatives questions related to applications for sewer and water availability, pre-treatment discharge, power generation, geological conditions, and cooling systems as well as permitted use versus conditional use guidelines. No opportunity for questions from the public was provided by the P&Z commission. Residents were advised they would be escorted from the meeting if they were found to be out of order, and some were asked to leave. According to TenKey, the proposed Franklin-Simpson Data Storage & Service Center (DSSC) by TenKey represents the next generation of data infrastructure, providing high-paying jobs, generating millions in tax revenue, and utilizing cutting-edge closed-loop cooling technology. TenKey advised the full development plan process will take several months to complete and involves various investigations, studies, and assessments to ensure compliance with local, state, and federal requirements.
