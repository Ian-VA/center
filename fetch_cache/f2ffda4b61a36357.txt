TRENTON, Ohio — A collective of concerned residents in Trenton is sounding off about a proposed data center that could transform 141 acres of land into a large-scale facility. Prologis, a California-based real estate company, approached the city to purchase the land near Trenton Industrial Park. The company made the purchase for about $7 million, according to a city official. Advertisement Tiffany Wilson, a resident of Trenton for more than 40 years, strongly opposes the project. "This city absolutely cannot accommodate a 1-million-square-foot data center," Wilson said. Others voiced their concerns about long-term impacts. "This approval process has many studies that need to be done: environmental health, sound, transportation, roadways," shared Barry Blankenship. Residents who spoke with WLWT question the approval process, claiming decisions were made with no public input. Mayor Ryan Perry defended the city's transparency, stating that this project was made public in 2024. “October of last year is the first time it went public," he shared. "It was always public when it went to the zoning board, and we also had a reading, a public forum at a council meeting before we voted on the final thing of this." Perry said Prologis' presence would be an added benefit to Trenton, bringing in 150 jobs and hundreds of thousands in tax dollars and water sales. Alexander Willan, who lives close to the industrial park, isn't convinced it has any place in town. “These are not and should not, in my opinion, be set anywhere near a residential area," he said, referencing a proposed solar farm in addition to the proposed data center. Mattie Sorrentino, a representative with Prologis, told WLWT the project is still in the early stages of planning. She said the area in Trenton is an optimal site for the following reasons: "First, this property sits within a manufacturing corridor and benefits from excellent proximity to reliable power. "Second, Prologis has a strong presence across Ohio, with teams and portfolios in the Cincinnati and Columbus areas. Working with the city of Trenton has been really positive and collaborative so far." Trenton City Council meets Thursday, Nov. 6, at 6:30 p.m. and 7:30 p.m., where residents can voice their concerns during public comment.
