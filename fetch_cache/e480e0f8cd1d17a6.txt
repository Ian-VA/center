News Release HOBART — Amazon is planning another major expansion in Indiana with a $15 billion commitment to build new data center campuses in the northern part of the state, a move expected to create about 1,100 permanent jobs and thousands of construction positions. One such campus in Hobart has already been announced, as work continues on the company’s separate $11 billion Amazon Web Services complex in New Carlisle. The company has not released specific locations or building counts for all the new campuses but confirmed the investment will add 2.4 gigawatts of data center capacity. Mayor Josh Huddleston of Hobart said that the town’s AWS project would create “close to a thousand” long-term positions. Officials say the facilities in Hobart and elsewhere will mirror the infrastructure used to support AWS’ Project Rainer AI supercomputer, which draws power from the New Carlisle site. The other new campuses are expected to be located within the seven-county northwest Indiana region. Amazon projects strong demand for technical workers, including engineers, network specialists and operations managers. Regional leaders say the investment will accelerate the area’s shift toward a data-driven economy and build on existing utility and transportation assets that have supported recent interest in large-scale technology projects. Another reason the prospect of data centers is appealing to local officials is potential tens of millions in revenue, which could offset recent losses brought by the Indiana General Assembly’s update on property tax code this year. Huddleston confirmed as much, saying the move keeps costs from being offset to residents. He added that Amazon is taking steps to prevent strain on local infrastructure, water or energy. The company is partnering with Northern Indiana Public Service Co. through its newly approved subsidiary, GenCo, to supply power for the campuses. Under the arrangement, Amazon will cover fees for using existing transmission lines and the costs of any new infrastructure. Utility officials estimate the structure will prevent rate increases for current customers and create long-term savings while adding three gigawatts of electric capacity to the regional grid. Amazon also plans to expand training programs tied to data center operations through partnerships with Ivy Tech Community College and other organizations.
