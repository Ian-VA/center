Updated: 10:35 PM CDT Mar 25, 2026 Editorial Standards ⓘ Emily Pofahl Reporter Residents near Microsoft's AI data center campus in Mount Pleasant earlier this month noticed a new noise radiating from the nearly complete center by 90th Street and County Road KR.A persistent hum, high in pitch but low in volume, that would turn on suddenly and sometimes last for days."It's just a high, kind of high-pitched drone sound," said David Ricchio, who lives about one mile from the center.Microsoft broke ground on the campus in 2023, and construction will continue in phases through at least 2028, but the first data center meant to help train new AI models will be fully operational later this year.It will be the first out of a series of mega-data centers focused on supporting artificial intelligence, going up around southeast Wisconsin to begin operations. More are still under construction or in planning phases in Port Washington, Beaver Dam and Kenosha. Donald Briggs, who lives by the Mount Pleasant data center campus, said he and his neighbors have become accustomed to the construction noise but are troubled by the new sound. "I can hear it inside my house, and that's what concerns me," Briggs said.The noise is faint, but audible, inside Briggs' living room.The hum appears to be coming from the southern part of the campus. It's not clear which part of the technology is causing the prolonged noise, but Briggs and Ricchio are concerned it will be part of the center's long-term operations. Microsoft plans to build more than 15 centers in Mount Pleasant. In a statement to 12 News, a Microsoft spokesperson said, "We're aware of the noise concerns raised about our Mount Pleasant facility, we're investigating the root cause, and hope to share an update soon." The company lists servers, vehicles, backup generators, and cooling equipment as the main sources of data center noise on its website, though the hum obviously isn't coming from traffic and, according to the website, server noise is only audible inside the data centers themselves.Briggs and Ricchio said much of the neighborhood isn't anti-data center. They are, however, pleading for some more communication from Microsoft and the contractors, Walsh Construction. Briggs said he has complained to Microsoft and to the city, but has not heard back about a solution."Actually, I got more success talking to you and Channel 12 than I got from them," he said.Ricchio said he's uncertain about whether he could continue living with the noise.
