Prince William County Facebook Twitter WhatsApp SMS Email Print Copy article link Save Facebook Twitter WhatsApp SMS Email Print Copy article link Save The Prince William Board of County Supervisors on Tuesday unanimously approved a data center rezoning that paves the way for the extension of the Iron Mountain data center campus. The VA 8/9 rezoning in the Brentsville District consists of just under 19 acres to be rezoned from office flex, planned mixed use district and a sliver of agricultural – along the old Hayden Road – to light industrial (M-2) for data center use. The property is located near Wellington Road and Sudley Manor Drive, just off Prince William County Parkway. The project had received a positive recommendation from county staff and a recommendation for approval from the Planning Commission by a 5-3 vote. Following the July 20 death of Bob Weir, the late Gainesville District supervisor and an ardent data center critic, the current board now consists of only seven members. The VA 8/9 application offered supervisors a dilemma: either deny the rezoning and allow the new data center buildings to be constructed by right, in closer proximity (75-100 feet closer) to the nearby Arcadia Run apartments across the street – which is possible because Iron Mountain already owns the adjacent road – or approve the project and maintain the proposed distance from the residential complex. The board ultimately opted for the latter, approving the proposal. The property is located within the county’s Data Center Opportunity Zone Overlay District. According to Jonelle Cameron, an attorney representing the applicant, Iron Mountain Data Centers, the VA 8/9 extension’s building-to-building distance from Arcadia Run will be 411 feet, with mechanical equipment capped at 15 feet away. Building height will be capped at 75 feet, including rooftop mechanical equipment. There will also be four electric vehicle charging stations on the premises – though Cameron indicated those would only be for private, employee use. Responding to a question from Brentsville Supervisor Tom Gordy, whose district includes the property, during Tuesday’s meeting, Cameron added the VA 8/9 extension would require 48 megawatts of power to be supplied by Iron Mountain’s existing internal substation – thus eliminating the need for the construction of an additional substation. Cameron also told Deshundra Jefferson, board chair, the applicant had worked with the county arborist and would be able to plant two-inch caliber trees with a maximum height of six feet to improve the local viewshed. The applicant has established a special, dedicated email address for Arcadia Run residents to contact Iron Mountain, according to Cameron, by which the community can voice any future concerns. Victor S. Angry, Neabsco District supervisor, twice said Iron Mountain representatives have been “good stewards” to the community since the company bought the land in 2017. Gordy ultimately initiated a motion to approve the rezoning, conveying his desire to “mitigate the impact” of the project and select the “lesser of [two] evils.” Jefferson, a data center critic in her own right, begrudgingly voted in favor of the proposal but lamented her “hesitation,” granting nonetheless that Iron Mountain has been “a good neighbor.” Jefferson added she didn’t want to “add insult to injury” and said she “has her reservations” regarding the project, requesting a future data center work session for the entire board in the coming months.
