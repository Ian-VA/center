Jon M. Williams' Riverview Innovation & Technology Campus outlined its plans for the River Road site in a filing with the town's Planning & Development Department, beginning the official review of the 440-page application. The Tonawanda Coke site as seen from the air Thursday. The developer has submitted a site-plan application seeking permission to construct a nearly 500,000-square-foot data center in the first of three project phases. Joed Viera/Buffalo News Williams has previously said a data center was an ideal reuse of the main Tonawanda Coke parcel, given its location and access to the electrical grid. Williams' Ontario Specialty Contracting has spent more than five years, at an expected cost of more than $100 million, cleaning up the site where, for a century, coal was converted to coke in a process that produced extensive pollution. The second phase, at a neighboring parcel, includes office buildings. The final phase, eyed for a piece of land on the other side of River Road, directly along the river, would have multifamily housing, commercial space and public access to the water. Tonawanda Supervisor Joseph Emminger said town officials are just beginning to review the data center proposal but he welcomed this next step in the process to revive the Tonawanda Coke property. "From my standpoint, if they can meet all their criteria that the town is going to place on them, if they can meet all that, it would be acceptable to me," Emminger told The Buffalo News on Thursday. But Bridge Rauch, environmental justice organizer for the Clean Air Coalition of Western New York, said data centers generate their own environmental issues, creating noise and other forms of pollution while using vast amounts of electricity. "This is not a good use of that site," Rauch said in an interview. Data center debated The filing from Riverview Innovation & Technology Campus doesn't reveal the anticipated cost of construction nor how many jobs the redevelopment would create. It also doesn't reveal which tech company, or companies, the data center would serve. 'Much better than it was': New life coming to polluted Tonawanda Coke site “We've basically taken every threat off the site,” developer Jon Williams said. “Now what's left is to get the site into a place where we can redevelop.” Emminger said he believes the data center would not mine cryptocurrency and, he noted, it would need to operate within the limits of the town's noise ordinance. "If there's anywhere in the Town of Tonawanda where a data center, a transactional data center, could be developed, it would be in that area," Emminger said, pointing to the site's distance from residential properties. The Clean Air Coalition, however, has previously highlighted Williams' ties to cryptocurrency mining operations in the area . And even if the data center didn't mine cryptocurrency, the project's negative effects would outweigh its benefits to the community, Rauch said. "It's part of this old extractive economy that we were trying to break away from," Rauch said. "It's like, I don't think Tonawanda residents would be thrilled to be paying more in energy prices to enrich some tech billionaires that have never even been to Western New York."
