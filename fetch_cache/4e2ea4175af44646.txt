The Three Rivers Regional Commission confirmed this week that its review of the project — known as Central Coweta Industrial Phase II (DRI #4374) — will include input only from affected parties as defined under Georgia’s Development of Regional Impact (DRI) rules. Those parties include nearby local governments, state agencies, and other regional commissions. In an email to The Newnan Times-Herald, Three Rivers Regional Planner Stephanie Wagner said the agency had adjusted its past practice of accepting citizen comments. “We’ve advised private citizens to send comments to their elected representatives,” Wagner wrote. “Although past DRI reviews had been open to citizen comments, we have adjusted the procedure.” Wagner said the change was made because private citizens do not meet the DRI definition of affected parties, and because of privacy and formatting concerns related to including personal comments in official reports. S he added that if any elected officials wish to include citizen comments, they can forward them for inclusion. Project details The proposal calls for the construction of eight data center buildings totaling about 2.4 million square feet on a 458-acre site located north of State Route 34/Bullsboro Drive, south of Coweta Industrial Parkway, east of U.S. 29, and west of Interstate 85. The developer, Central Coweta Industrial LLC, lists a Senoia address and email linked to Brent Scarbrough & Company and its parent firm, Brent Holdings. According to documents submitted to the commission, the development would also include three electrical substations, stormwater infrastructure, and internal access roads. The buildout is expected to be completed by 2028 for this phase, with the traffic study evaluating 2035 conditions for full build analysis. The project’s estimated total value is $4 billion, with projected annual local tax revenues of $40 million. Road and environmental details The study found that several intersections in the area, particularly along SR 154, SR 14/U.S. 29, and Amlajack Boulevard, already operate at or near capacity during peak hours. The report recommends new turn lanes and minor intersection improvements but no major off-site road changes. Roughly 20 percent of the site would be impervious surface once built out, with stormwater facilities designed for detention, water quality, and channel protection. Developers report no expected displacement of existing land uses or generation of hazardous waste. Location and context The proposed site sits between Amlajack Boulevard and Coweta Industrial Parkway, in an area with existing industrial operations. The application seeks industrial “M” zoning as part of the rezoning that triggered the DRI review. Under the new DRI communication rules, citizens may send their concerns to Coweta County commissioners or other elected officials, who can forward them to the Three Rivers Regional Commission for inclusion in the advisory report.
