Tesla did not initially provide a reason, although Bloomberg has reported that the project’s lead is leaving the company. Musk later clarified in a post on X that the decision was made to streamline Tesla's AI chip research. Last August, the governor’s office announced — alongside the University at Buffalo's supercomputer — that Dojo would bring a $500 million investment to Buffalo, positioning the city as a destination for supercomputing. A spokesperson for Empire State Development told 2 On Your Side , "New York State has not invested in the Dojo supercomputer project at the Buffalo Gigafactory. As of Feb. 1, Tesla reported investing $314 million in their supercomputer in Buffalo. Any update since that time would have to be addressed to Tesla directly." Tesla is in the middle of renegotiating its lease agreement with New York State for the South Buffalo plant, with its current deal set to expire in the coming years.
