MIDDLESEX TOWNSHIP, Pa. — A proposed data center complex in Middlesex Township, Pennsylvania, is sparking debate among residents and officials. The $15 billion project, known as Pennsylvania Digital 1 , or PAX-1, would span 700 acres off Country Club Road and include 18 large housing units for data storage. "I would be out here shoveling snow in my driveway in the winter. You would see deer coming up through the snow," said Bob Hess, a 41-year resident of Middlesex Township. Advertisement The project, a partnership between Pennsylvania Data Centers and PowerHouse Data Centers , promises significant economic advantages. Developers estimate it will create 450 jobs, lower traffic impact than other types of developments, and generate $65 million in tax benefits for Cumberland County, Middlesex Township, and the Cumberland Valley School District. "These data centers are massive, and they are going to have an extreme impact," said Cumberland County Commissioner Gary Eichelberger. "The areas that are where these are being considered, they are the single largest impact on land use and, just from their sheer size, but also the consumption of natural resources," Eichelberger added. The proposed PAX-1 site is strategically located near significant power lines and would include its own PPL substation. However, questions remain about where the required power and immense water supply would come from. "We need to be transparent about what these issues are. We need to be out there having this public discussion," Eichelberger said. "Pay some attention to the environment. Don't come in and just try to make a profit, and to be a good neighbor," Hess said. Eichelberger tells News 8 PAX-1 presents an opportunity for township residents to have their voices heard. In a Cumberland County initiative, the Land Partnerships Plan , leaders ask residents to give their feedback to their plan through Sept. 5, 2025. As for those in Middlesex Township, scheduled planning commission meetings at 7 p.m. on Aug. 11 and Aug. 25, as well as on Sept. 8 and Sept. 22, will be open for public comment on PAX-1, according to township zoning officer Mark Carpenter.
