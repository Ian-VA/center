Share this article paywall-free Email Copy Link A large-scale artificial intelligence data center campus is planned for more than 1,000 acres in Angelina County near Paper Mill Creek, according to county property records, company materials and local officials. Amp Z says it is leading the development of a “gigawatt-scale data center campus in Deep East Texas,” built to serve hyperscale users with both immediate electrical capacity and room for future expansion. The company states the campus will span more than 1,000 acres, with nearby land available that could bring the total project area to about 4,000 acres. A memorandum of option recorded in Angelina County shows Verdant Industries, LLC, and Verdant Industries Realty, LLC, granted Amp Z Champion Data Center Holdings LLC the right to purchase approximately 1,041 acres across multiple abstracts and surveys at the former Southland Paper Mill. In a public statement released Thursday, the city of Lufkin said the project remains under development and negotiation but could represent an estimated $1.05 billion in private capital investment if finalized. Current projections indicate the development could support roughly 500 construction jobs over more than four years and about 30 full-time operational positions once completed. The city also described the property as an established heavy industrial site that historically supported large-scale paper mill operations. Redevelopment plans call for removing obsolete structures and constructing modern, warehouse-style data center buildings. Project information released by the company indicates the site already has an active onsite electric substation capable of delivering 55 megawatts of power, with plans to scale beyond 2.1 gigawatts through a combination of utility service and onsite generation by 2029. Company materials also state the location has access to roughly 20 million gallons of water per day. According to the city, project representatives estimate the facility would use approximately 500 gallons per day, comparable to the usage of three homes and only a small fraction of the system’s available capacity. Lufkin Mayor Mark Hicks described the proposal as transformational in scale. “It is truly a once-in-a-lifetime project for Angelina County,” Hicks said. “I mean, it’s in the billions of dollars in terms of investment. It’s just a real opportunity for us because for years, people have talked about, ‘We've got to find somebody to come in here that can use our water.’ Angelina County has access to so much water, and they would make use of our water — not to the point of draining our resources or anything like that.” Hicks said the city maintains control of its water supply and would sell water to the facility, which he described as a potential financial benefit for Lufkin. “The city of Lufkin controls all that water, and they would buy it from the city, which would be a boost for the city,” he said. “But all the way around, it’s a great opportunity.” Hicks also framed the project as a potential step toward rebuilding portions of the area’s historic industrial background. He said the development could provide “a pathway for us to move back” toward the type of industrial and corporate job presence the region saw decades ago. Company materials describe the location as having several logistical characteristics often sought for large infrastructure projects, including a setting in an unincorporated portion of the county with no building code or zoning restrictions and partially completed environmental studies. Hicks acknowledged data centers have faced scrutiny in some communities but emphasized the site’s industrial history and distance from residential neighborhoods. “There’s some negative stories out there about data centers. But it’s at a paper mill site that is just in terrible condition," he said, calling it "like Chernobyl out there." "It’s not near residential, so it’s not going to have any more effects than that paper mill had," he added. Additional information provided by Oncor Electric Delivery outlines the process required before large customers can connect to the power grid. Prospective customers must sign an interconnection agreement and provide financial collateral to ensure existing ratepayers are not financially impacted if a project does not move forward Amp Z states the property is eligible for Opportunity Zone incentives, Foreign Trade Zone designation and potential Texas data center tax abatements. No construction timeline has been announced, and discussions related to the proposal remain ongoing, according to the city. Ruben Ibarra Jr.'s email address is ruben.ibarra@lufkindailynews.com .
