INDEPENDENCE, Mo. — There's been progress on the construction of a more than 2 million-square-foot data center in eastern Independence. Tuesday night, the Independence City Council approved a plan for Nebius, the European company investing in the center, to buy electricity to operate phase one of the center, expected to open in the fall. Advertisement Heavy equipment operators have been grading acreage in the Little Blue Valley Parkway, Highway 78 area for the past month in preparation for the new center and a new power plant to provide electricity for it. Howard Hoff will have a clear view of it from his home on North Bly Road where he's been living for nearly 30 years. "Oh, we're not happy about it. But you know the way it sounds, if the City of Independence wants it, they're going to get it," he said. Wednesday, the data center was a topic of discussion at the Independence Chamber of Commerce luncheon with Independence Power and Light (IPL) Director Joe Hegendeffer as the special guest. Hegendeffer said Nebius is paying for the new power plant, so the rest of the city won't be impacted. And he said the data center won't affect residents' electric bills. "That's probably the most common question because we've seen that in other communities, right. So, the way that this deal is structured is there's going to be pass through pricing. So, the energy prices today may not be the energy prices in 5 or 10 years. Well, that's the price that this data center will pay for the energy that it uses from today and in the future," Hegendeffer said. He also said that because of the data center's closed-loop cooling system, excessive water usage won't be a problem. Councilman Jared Fears said he's not completely sold on the project yet because he's waiting to hear from Nebius about how much noise the plant will produce. "I've heard a number of questions from residents, and primarily they've been around environmental issues and noise," he said. Hoff said he moved to his home in the 1990s because, while it's still in the city, it's more like the country. One of his neighbors is a bald eagle. Its nest is just south of his home and very near the project site. "I hope it won't hurt the property value, but I got a feeling it will," Hoff said. A city council vote is expected in March to give Nebius property and sales tax breaks during the construction phase of the project, which is expected to take three years. The development is expected to bring tens of millions of tax dollars to the city of Independence beginning in 2028.
