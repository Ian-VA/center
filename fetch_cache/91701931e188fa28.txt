The Real Story – A Column By Dirk Knudsen Data Centers – they are absolutely taking over Washington County. Particularly, Hillsboro, Oregon. This is the 43rd story we’ve published on the subject. When the land is gone and the jobs are gone, the record will show that the Herald saw it coming. And while land speculators and deal brokers are rolling in money every time another acre is plowed over, the rest of us are suffering under job losses caused by the AI that is powered inside these buildings. It is fair to say at this point that the Data Centers inside the City of Hillsboro and the AI that they serve up will be responsible for countless thousands of jobs in the next 5 to 10 years. Let that sink in. We have that many data centers here (3rd highest concentration in the country in any one place), and their reach is so vast using the fiber optic highway running through our valley, that jobs across the Western US will be affected. Today, Flexential, a massive player in the Data Center space Nationwide, “ Hillsboro has become a smart alternative to Silicon Valley for customers seeking the performance, flexibility, and cost structure they can’t find elsewhere — especially those with strong ties across the Pacific,” said Ryan Mallory , President and COO of Flexential. “This project reflects our belief in focused, right-sized development that enables customer growth without chasing unnecessary scale, and it furthers our commitment to delivering reliable solutions while introducing much-needed capacity in a highly coveted area.” Flexential operates four data centers in Hillsboro , with Hillsboro 5 — a 358,000-square-foot, 36 MW facility — under construction and expected to come online next year. Together, they form a major transpacific interconnection hub, with Hillsboro 2 often referred to as the “ Network Access Point of the Northwest ” because it houses the densest connectivity hub in the region and the U.S. landings of the New Cross Pacific and Hawaiki subsea cables, among four other subsea cables. The new building will be Flexential 6. From the Flexential Press Release Today: https://www.flexential.com/resources/press-release/flexential-acquires-prime-property-hillsboro-plans-new-data-center In fact, in 2023 and 2024, the Governor, pushed by Senator Janeen Sollman , Hillsboro Mayor Steve Callaway , North Plains Mayor Teri Lenahen , and a massive contingent of land owners and policymakers, tried to expand Industrial Land expansions onto thousands of acres of pristine farmland. The CHIPS ACT was going to pay out Billions into Washington County, and we would be the hottest tech area in the Country. Guess what, that era is ending, and if Intel doesn’t pull off a Hail Mary, it’s over. The building will be massive, but inside it will house racks of servers, not employees. The parking lots will be nearly empty. Power Hungry, Job Light Flexential’s own figures show that the new Hillsboro 6 will support 27 megawatts of computing power. That translates to ~236 gigawatt-hours a year — the same as 21,300 Oregon homes . But while it takes the power of a small city, a facility this size employs only 30–50 people . If the current trend continues, Hillsboro could see 25 large data centers running 24 hours a day in the next five years . Each averages 20–30 MW. Collectively, that’s 500–750 MW of continuous demand — rivaling the daily consumption of all of Washington County and its 615,000 residents. Who Pays the Bill? PGE raised residential electricity rates by ~5.5% in January 2025 . Bills are ~50% higher than five years ago . By contrast, large industrial users, such as data centers, negotiate rates that can be a third of what households pay per kilowatt-hour. That rate disparity sparked the POWER Act (HB 3546) , passed this summer, which creates a new rate class for data centers so their grid expansion costs don’t automatically land on household bills. But enforcement is thin, and the cost of substations, transformers, and new transmission still flows into the same system we all rely on. The Scale of AI in Hillsboro If 25 hyperscale facilities come online here, Hillsboro will be hosting: ~162,500 AI servers Over 1.6 million AI chips (GPUs/TPUs) 900 battery containers (3 MWh each, for backup power) 7,750 potential jobs were erased compared with historic manufacturing footprints. Every Data Center that goes in forever removes our capacity to hire humans. In this case, a perfect location for people to work on US 26, surrounded by retail, restaurants, neighborhoods, parks, and more, will be lost forever.
